Same sales, different growth: YoY depends on the currency

Sales are recorded in DKK. Each year is converted at that year's average rate (DKK per 100 units), as the DAX measure does with LOOKUPVALUE. When a currency strengthens against DKK, growth in that currency looks smaller.

Time intelligence

Months come from the company's week calendar (4-4-5 weeks per quarter), not calendar dates. In 2020 the extra week moves week 48, the Black Friday week, from December into November, so compare Nov + Dec together. In Power BI these are DAX measures (DATESYTD, DATEADD).

What the data says

1 · 2020 grew 9.4% — but a third of that is an extra week

2020 sales were 2.71B DKK vs 2.48B in 2019 (+9.4%). 2020 has 53 ISO weeks; week 53 alone is 71M DKK. On a comparable 52 weeks, growth is +6.5%. Units grew only +1.1% on 52 weeks, so growth came from a higher average price (161 → 170 DKK), not from more pieces sold.

2 · The peak moved earlier

Q4 is almost half of the year (48% of 2020 sales). In 2020, weeks 44–48 (up to and including Black Friday week) were +25% vs 2019, while weeks 49–52 were −19%. The monthly view shows "November +85%, December −25%", which is mostly the week calendar moving Black Friday week into November.

3 · Charms are the business, but growth came from elsewhere

Charms are 67% of 2020 sales and were flat (−0.3%). Bracelets (+41%) and Dangles (+47%) delivered the growth. Dangles and Necklaces barely existed in 2018 and are new lines.

4 · Fast product turnover

62% of 2020 sales came from products launched in 2020 and 33% from 2019 launches. Almost nothing older than two years still sells. 31 of 272 selling products make 80% of sales, and the top product (P 84) alone is 7.6%.

5 · Two markets and one business line dominate

Australia (31%) and China (31%) make 62% of sales. Business line BL 1 in China sells 27% of all units, about 3× the next one. The USA (+16%) and Spain (+12%) grew fastest; Australia was slowest (+7%).

Recommendation

Report growth on comparable weeks and a fixed currency. Plan inventory and marketing around a Black-Friday-led peak, not December. Keep the launch pipeline strong, since sales depend on products less than two years old. Reduce dependence on BL 1 by growing the USA and Spain.

How it was built

1. Data audit and Power Query

  • 542,605 weekly rows. Removed 5,480 rows with invalid keys (# business line, ' product).
  • Kept units as decimals (the source has fractional units; a whole-number type would round them).
  • Parsed launch dates written as dd.mm.yyyy and list prices with a Danish decimal format; # prices set to 0.
  • Product 99 sells (198K DKK) but is missing from the product list, so it shows as unknown.

2. Star schema

One fact table (FactSalesOverview) with DimProduct, DimBusinessLine, a week calendar that maps ISO week → month, and a DimDate date table. The yearly exchange rates link to DimDate and a DimCurrency slicer table.

3. DAX measures

  • Currency conversion: look up the selected currency's rate for the year (LOOKUPVALUE) and divide the DKK amount by rate ÷ 100
  • Units, ASP = Sales ÷ Units
  • YTD / LY / LM, YoY % and MoM % with DATESYTD and DATEADD

4. Report

Cover, overview, time-intelligence page with country bookmarks, sales-details page with a map, Top/Bottom N and a report-page tooltip. This web version recomputes the same measures in the browser from an aggregated extract (data.json).
